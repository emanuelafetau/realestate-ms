# Scenarios
---

## Sara — Real Estate Agent

Sara is a real estate agent at a property agency in Vlora, Albania. She manages around 20 active clients at any given time and handles everything from the first phone call to the final contract signing. She is always on the move — meeting clients, visiting properties, and attending viewings — and relies heavily on her phone to stay organised throughout the day.

Sara receives a lot of new leads through the agency website, but by the time she gets back to the office after a morning of viewings, some of those leads have already called a competitor. She needs a way to be notified the moment a new lead comes in so she can respond immediately, even when she is not at her desk.

When she does speak to a client, she takes notes on paper and then struggles to remember the details later — what their budget was, which area they preferred, how many bedrooms they needed. She wants to log these preferences directly into the system during or after the call so that the next time she speaks to that client, everything is already there in front of her.

Sara also manages property viewings manually, using a personal calendar that her colleagues cannot see. Double bookings happen more often than she would like. She wants to schedule viewings inside the shared system so that conflicts are flagged automatically and clients receive confirmation and reminders without her having to send them separately.

When a client makes an offer, Sara currently tracks the negotiation over email. She has lost track of counter offers before, which has caused embarrassing moments with clients. She needs a place to log every offer and see the full negotiation trail clearly, so she always knows exactly where a deal stands.

At the end of a successful deal, Sara has to manually calculate her commission and then check with the accounts team to make sure the number is right. She wants the system to do this for her automatically the moment a deal is closed, so she can trust the figure without going back and forth.

## Marko — Senior Real Estate Agent

Marko is one of the most experienced agents at the agency with over 15 years in the industry. He built his client base entirely through personal relationships and is proud that many of his clients come back to him years later when they are ready to buy again. He is not a heavy technology user — he still keeps a paper notebook — but he is open to using a system if it genuinely saves him time.

Marko's biggest problem is long-term clients. He meets a couple who are interested in buying but are not ready for another four months. He makes a mental note to follow up, but four months is a long time. By the time he remembers, they have already signed with someone else. He needs the system to remind him when it is time to reach out to a client again, with enough context to pick up the conversation as if no time has passed.

He also manages several properties that have been sitting on the market for a long time. He is not always aware of how long until an owner calls to complain. He needs the system to alert him when a property he is responsible for has not had any viewings or activity for more than a set number of days, so he can take action before the relationship with the owner becomes strained.

## Diana — Agency Manager

Diana is the manager of the real estate agency and is responsible for a team of eight agents. She is under pressure from the agency owner to deliver consistent revenue growth every month and is expected to know exactly how the team is performing at any given time.

Her current problem is visibility. She only finds out that an agent is struggling when the month is almost over and the numbers do not add up. By then it is too late to make a real difference. She wants a dashboard that shows her every agent's activity — how many leads they have contacted, how many viewings they have completed, and how many deals they are close to closing — so she can step in early when someone is falling behind.

Diana also needs to set monthly targets for each agent individually. Right now targets are communicated verbally in team meetings and not everyone remembers them the same way. She wants to set them formally inside the system so that each agent can see their own target and track their own progress without having to ask her.

At the end of each month, Diana spends two days pulling together revenue numbers from spreadsheets, email threads, and handwritten notes to produce a report for the agency owner. She needs the system to generate this report automatically so she can spend that time managing her team instead.

## Lisa — System Administrator

Lisa is the office administrator and the person responsible for managing the agency's internal systems. She is methodical and detail-oriented and is the first person anyone calls when something technical goes wrong.

When a new agent joins the agency, Lisa currently has to set up their email, explain the tools they need to use, and manually hand over login details for several different systems. It takes most of a morning and still results in the new agent not knowing where to start. She wants to create a single account in the system, assign the right role, and have everything — including a welcome email with login details — handled automatically so the agent can start working on their first day without waiting for her.

When an agent leaves the agency, Lisa needs to make sure they can no longer access client data, property records, or any sensitive information. She needs to deactivate their account immediately and be confident that the access is fully revoked the moment she does so.

Lisa is also responsible for accountability in the agency. If a client record is changed incorrectly, or a document goes missing, she needs to be able to trace exactly who made the change and when. She needs the system to maintain a full audit log of every action so that nothing can be altered or deleted without a trace.

## James — Property Buyer

James is a software engineer who has recently relocated to Tirana from the UK and is looking to buy his first property in Albania. He is unfamiliar with the local property market and the buying process, and is relying heavily on his agent to guide him through every step.

His biggest fear is being kept in the dark. He has heard stories from friends about offers sitting in limbo for weeks with no communication from the agency. He needs to be notified every time the status of his offer changes — whether it has been accepted, countered, or rejected — so he always knows what is happening without having to chase his agent for an update.

James also wants to make sure the properties he is shown are actually relevant to what he has asked for. He has already attended two viewings for properties that were clearly outside his stated budget. He wants his preferences — budget, location, number of bedrooms — to be properly recorded so that the suggestions he receives are ones worth his time.

When a viewing is confirmed, James wants to receive a reminder the day before so he does not miss it. He is juggling a new job and a new city, and without a reminder he risks forgetting appointments that he was genuinely looking forward to attending.
